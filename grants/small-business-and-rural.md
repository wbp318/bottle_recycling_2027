# Small business, rural development, workforce, and tourism

Money for the LLC as a business, whatever it does. Researched October 2026. **(snippet)** marks a fact seen only in a search summary; **(est.)** marks our estimate.

## The honest version

- **True grants for a for-profit startup are rare.** The few that exist reach the LLC only through a town (CDBG Micro-Enterprise, which also tests income) or never reach it at all (tourism grants, NBRC, USDA RBDG go to towns and nonprofits).
- **The usable money is loans, loan guarantees, and free advising.** The standard path is a local bank loan backed by FAME loan insurance or an SBA guarantee, with gap money from CEI or the regional council of governments.
- **The partner living out of state is fine.** Citizenship is what matters now:
  - **SBA 7(a) and 504 (since March 1, 2026):** every owner must be a U.S. citizen or national whose main home is in the U.S. A green-card holder now disqualifies the business.
  - **SBA Microloans:** the same rule since April 1, 2026.
  - **USDA B&I** still allows 51% citizen or permanent-resident ownership.
- **Income-tested programs:** CDBG Micro-Enterprise tests the household income of all owners, so the funding partner's income likely rules the LLC out (est.).

## Notes on the top picks

**SBDC first.** Free one-on-one help with the business plan, projections, and packaging the loan request. It's also required before applying for a CDBG micro-grant. In 2025 it served 2,076 clients ([Mainebiz](https://mainebiz.biz/article/maine-small-business-centers-helped-2076-clients-in-2025/)).

**FAME Commercial Loan Insurance.** This insures a bank's loan so the bank will make it; it's not a loan from FAME. A fast-track version covers up to 75% with near-immediate approval. Expect FAME to want guarantees and financials from both partners (est.).

**SBA rules tightened in 2025–26** ([CDC New England](https://cdcnewengland.com/post/new-sba-citizenship-requirements-what-bankers-and-borrowers-need-to-know); [Phillips Lytle](https://phillipslytle.com/the-sba-reverts-back-to-stricter-lending-standards/)):

- Startups must put in at least 10% equity, and SBA checks where it came from.
- Loans over $50k need collateral.
- Every owner must be a U.S. citizen (see above).
