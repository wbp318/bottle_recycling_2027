# Winter boat and RV storage

**Rank 1.** The cheapest idea with the least labor, and every customer signs a contract.

## What it is

Fenced outdoor storage on gravel or field for boats on trailers and for RVs, roughly October to May.

Add-ons that raise revenue per customer:

- Shrink-wrapping boats in the fall
- Winterizing engines and water systems (or referring it out)
- Haul-out and launch with the farm tractor
- Later, indoor storage in a barn or hoop building

## How it makes money

Customers pay per foot or per unit for the season, in advance.

**Estimates for an outdoor lot (est.):**

- Boats: $5–10 per foot for the season
- RVs: $400–900 for the season
- 40 units: roughly $15k–35k gross per season

## Permits

- **State:** We found no Maine state license for boat or vehicle storage.
- **Town:** Zoning and possibly site-plan review. This varies by town, so call the code enforcement officer first.
- **Sales tax:** We could not confirm whether storage is taxable in Maine. Ask Maine Revenue Services.

## Risks

- **Current-use tax.** If the storage area is on acres enrolled in the Farmland program, converting them can trigger a withdrawal penalty ([36 M.R.S. §1112-C](https://legislature.maine.gov/statutes/36/title36sec1112-C.pdf)).
- **Liability.** As the holder of other people's property, the farm owes them care (bailment). Use a written contract that limits liability and requires the owner's own insurance.
- Fire, theft, fluid leaks onto farmland, and keeping access plowed in winter.

## How it fits the redemption center

It uses land, not the sorting building, and earns in winter. The gate, cameras and signage can serve both, and storage customers are local households that also redeem containers.
